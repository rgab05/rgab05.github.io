How this model works

Total demand follows a simplified Kaya identity: energy = a GDP effect (elasticity 0.55 — demand grows slower than GDP as economies become less energy-intensive), a population effect (elasticity 0.25), and an autonomous efficiency effect (–1.5%/yr, faster under higher renewable ambition). The three sliders control this global total and how fast its mix shifts toward wind, solar, hydro, nuclear and bioenergy; fossil fuels absorb whatever demand is left.

Regions are built by splitting each source's global total across five blocs (US, China, EU, India, rest-of-world) using shares calibrated to known 2000–2023 patterns — e.g. China's rising share of coal and solar, the EU's early wind lead giving way to China, India's still-small but fast-growing base — extrapolated forward with a saturating trend so no bloc's share runs away to an extreme. India and the rest-of-world are also given a modest extra demand-growth allowance, reflecting continued industrialization even under an aggressive global efficiency scenario. The regional split is an illustrative estimate built for this tool, not an official country-by-country dataset — treat it as directionally reasonable rather than precise.

A note on totals: the ~680 EJ figure shown for 2023 is higher than the ~620 EJ headline you may see quoted from the Energy Institute's Statistical Review, because this model adds a bioenergy category (modern biofuels/biopower plus traditional biomass burned for cooking and heat) that the EI/BP-style accounting excludes. Coal, oil, gas, nuclear, hydro, wind and solar match the EI convention (input-equivalent method for non-fossil electricity).
